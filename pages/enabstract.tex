% English Abstract

Although hardware encoders like NVENC offer extremely low latency and high throughput, severe perceptual quality degradation at low bitrates—such as blocking and blurring—limits their application in real-time scenarios like live streaming. Meanwhile, mainstream deep learning enhancement models, despite excellent performance, are too computationally expensive to embed into real-time pipelines.

To address these issues, this thesis proposes a reinforcement learning-based video coding co-processing solution. Built around the NVENC baseline, the system introduces a lightweight Rate-Adaptive Pre-processing Network (RAPNet) for intelligent video enhancement prior to encoding. The Actor-Critic architecture employs a residual learning strategy for fine-grained original frame corrections. A compound reward function based on relative rate-distortion gain achieves effective trade-offs between perceptual quality and bitrate. Furthermore, a frequency-aware composite loss strategy, comprising weighted MSE, high-frequency suppression, and total variation loss, is proposed to suppress artifacts.

Comprehensive experiments on five UVG 1080p sequences validate the approach. Under the current experimental setup, the proposed RAPNet achieves an average BD-LPIPS gain of -9.48\% with only 139K parameters and an average pre-processing overhead of 5.4 ms per frame, accounting for about 12\% of the end-to-end latency. These results demonstrate that introducing lightweight pre-processing into an NVENC-based real-time coding pipeline can effectively reduce perceptual rate-distortion cost while preserving millisecond-level deployment overhead.

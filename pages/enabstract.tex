% English Abstract

Although hardware encoders like NVENC offer extremely low latency and high throughput, severe perceptual quality degradation—including blocking artifacts, ringing, and texture blurring—at low bitrates limits their application in real-time scenarios such as live streaming and cloud gaming. Meanwhile, mainstream deep learning enhancement models, despite their strong perceptual restoration performance, typically require tens of millions of parameters and hundreds of milliseconds of inference time, making them impractical for real-time encoding pipelines.

To address these issues, this thesis proposes a reinforcement learning-based video coding co-processing solution. Using the NVENC hardware encoder as the baseline, the system introduces a lightweight Rate-Adaptive Pre-processing Network (RAPNet) for intelligent video enhancement prior to encoding. Based on the Actor-Critic framework, the system employs a residual learning strategy to constrain the action space to fine-grained frame-level corrections, and designs a compound reward function formulated on relative rate-distortion gain to balance perceptual quality and transmission bitrate. Furthermore, a frequency-aware composite loss strategy—comprising weighted MSE loss, non-edge high-frequency suppression loss, and total variation smoothing loss—is proposed to suppress high-frequency artifacts introduced during enhancement.

Comprehensive experiments on five UVG 1080p sequences validate the approach. Under the experimental setup described in this thesis, the proposed RAPNet achieves an average BD-LPIPS gain of $-9.48\%$ with only 113K parameters and an average pre-processing overhead of 5.4 ms per frame, accounting for approximately 12\% of the end-to-end latency. These results demonstrate that introducing lightweight pre-processing into an NVENC-based real-time encoding pipeline can effectively reduce the perceptual rate-distortion cost while maintaining only millisecond-level additional latency.
